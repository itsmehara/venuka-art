About Venuka

Unleashing Creativity, One Stroke at a Time: Nurturing Unique Talents in the Heart of Kids

A creative journey

Venuka’s drawings began the way most children’s drawings do — a pencil, a blank page, and an idea that would not sit still. What made it grow was sharing it. Her first drawing video, a cow, went up on YouTube in November 2023. More followed almost every week.

On her first website she wrote about the videos herself:

“In this collection, I showcase a series of engaging and educational kids’ drawing tutorials, featuring delightful drawings of animals like ducks, giraffes, cows, and crawling babies, crafted through a fun and easy step-by-step process, creating colorful and adorable artifacts for young artists to enjoy and learn from.”

— Yours Venuka

Loving drawing and colouring

The drawing is only half of it. The colouring is where each picture really arrives — a green elephant, a rainbow parrot tail, a pig in three shades of pink. Venuka takes her time over it, and the videos show that: pencil lines first, colours after.

Animals, nature and characters

Look through the gallery and a pattern appears. Owls, cows, sheep, ducks, frogs, a snail and a tortoise, a giraffe, a parrot, a pig. Then the characters — a mermaid, a crawling baby — and whole small scenes with a pen, a farmyard and a sky.

A message from the family

The line at the top of every page came from Venuka’s dad, and it is still the best description of what this website is for:

“Unleashing Creativity, One Stroke at a Time: Nurturing Unique Talents in the Heart of Kids!”

— By My Dad

The videos are made together. As Venuka put it: “Teamed up with Dad to bring these videos to life, adding an extra layer of creativity to each drawing.”

📝 Space kept for more

Nothing on this page has been invented. If there is more the family would like visitors to know — how Venuka started, a favourite drawing, a photograph of her at work — there is room here for it, and it can be added at any time.

Why it matters

What drawing gives a child

Every finished page is practice at something bigger than the picture itself.

💭

Imagination

A blank page has no instructions. Deciding what goes on it — and what colour it should be — is imagination doing push-ups.

⌛

Patience

Colouring a whole elephant takes longer than it looks. Children learn to stay with something until it is properly finished.

🌟

Confidence

“I made that.” Holding up a finished drawing does something for a child that praise alone cannot.

🌈

Creativity

Once a child knows a cow can be pink, the whole world opens up a little.
